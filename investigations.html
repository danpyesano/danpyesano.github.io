Selected investigations

Investigations from my work as a journalist and investigator in Southeast Asia and the Middle East, on corporate power, natural resources and accountability.

Global Witness · 2016 · Lead investigator

Hostile Takeover: The corporate empire of Cambodia’s ruling family

Followed the paper trail through Cambodia’s company registry to map the business empire of Prime Minister Hun Sen’s family: 114 domestic companies, more than US$200 million in listed capital, and interests in almost every major sector of the economy.

The Phnom Penh Post · 2014–15 · Lead reporter

The calculus of logging

Exposed how a well-connected tycoon’s company allegedly used a reservoir-clearing permit as cover to strip protected rosewood worth an estimated US$300 million from the Cardamom Mountains, then traced the timber out of the country through leaked port data. SOPA Honourable Mention, 2015.

With May Titthara.

New Internationalist · 2013 · Reporter

The case of Ban Mae Surin

Challenged the official account of a March 2013 fire that killed 37 refugees in a camp on the Thai–Myanmar border, blamed on a cooking accident. Gathered testimony from more than 20 witnesses and put the camp’s own records before international criminal investigation experts.

With Saw C Rogers.

Financial Times · 2016 · Reporter

The dark shadow of Myanmar’s jade trade

Followed the money out of Myanmar’s jade trade. Receipts suggested politicians and industrialists had taken money from a US$96 million auction levy, while the state regulator held more than US$200 million in unscrutinised “other accounts”. Later cited by the UN Fact-Finding Mission on Myanmar.

The Sunday Telegraph · 2011 · Correspondent

Inside Syria’s ruling family

Reported from Damascus in the first weeks of the uprising. Former senior officials and members of the Assad family dismantled the image of Bashar al-Assad as a reformer held back by hardliners, casting the president himself as a main obstacle to change.

All reporting from Syria, credited anonymously for security reasons. Bylined to Loveday Morris.

The Phnom Penh Post · 2014 · Reporter

Friends in high places

Revealed a Chinese developer racing to build a US$5 billion resort inside Ream National Park, four years after a royal decree had supposedly revoked its licences. Ministry records showed state cooperation had grown, not stopped, helped by a “military-commerce alliance” with Hun Sen’s Bodyguard Unit.
