Selected investigations

Investigations from my work as a journalist and investigator in Southeast Asia and the Middle East, on corporate power, natural resources and accountability.

Global Witness · 2016 · Lead investigator

Hostile Takeover: The corporate empire of Cambodia’s ruling family

Mapped the business interests of Prime Minister Hun Sen’s family using records from Cambodia’s Ministry of Commerce company registry. The report linked family members to 114 private domestic companies with combined listed capital of more than US$200 million, across most key sectors of the economy.

The Phnom Penh Post · 2014–2015 · Lead reporter

The Try Pheap Group and Cambodia’s rosewood trade

A two-year series with May Titthara, built on a leaked conservation report, licence data and fieldwork in the Cardamom Mountains. It showed how the tycoon’s company allegedly used a reservoir-clearing permit as cover to move protected rosewood worth an estimated US$300 million, and traced exports through leaked port data. SOPA Honourable Mention, 2015.

New Internationalist · 2013 · Reporter

The case of Ban Mae Surin: tragic accident or foul play?

Investigated the March 2013 fire that killed 37 refugees at a camp in north-west Thailand near the Myanmar border, which officials initially blamed on a cooking accident. With Saw C Rogers, I interviewed more than 20 witnesses and took the camp’s own documentation of the fire to international criminal-investigation experts.

Financial Times · 2016 · Reporter

The dark shadow of Myanmar’s jade trade

Traced how money from Myanmar’s jade trade escaped public scrutiny. Reviewed receipts suggesting that politicians and industrialists took money from a US$96 million levy on the state jade auction, and reported that the state regulator kept more than US$200 million of gemstone revenue in unscrutinised “other accounts”. Later cited by the UN Fact-Finding Mission on Myanmar in its 2019 report on the military’s economic interests.

The Sunday Telegraph · 2011 · Special correspondent, Damascus

Middle East crisis: Inside Syria’s ruling family

Weeks into the 2011 uprising, former senior officials and members of the Assad family told the paper that the western view of Bashar al-Assad as a reformer held back by hardliners was a fiction fostered by the regime, and that the president was himself one of the main obstacles to change. Bylined to Loveday Morris; I contributed all the reporting from Syria, credited as a correspondent in Damascus who could not be named for security reasons.

The Phnom Penh Post · 2014 · Reporter

Friends in high places

Revealed that a Chinese developer with close ties to Hun Sen’s Bodyguard Unit was speeding up construction of a US$5 billion resort inside Ream National Park, four years after a royal decree had supposedly revoked its licences. Company records, a 2011 Ministry of Environment report and the prime minister’s own letter of support showed that government cooperation had increased, not stopped.
